## Phase 1 — Core I/O and model

### 1.1 Image buffers and tiling

- [x] `Image<T>` over u8, u16 and f32 with 1 to 4 channels, a row stride and a color-space tag (linear or sRGB-encoded, optional ICC profile), done in 0.3; the rest of 1.1 builds on it
- [x] Conversions between sample types and between encoded and linear, vectorized and parallel with rayon; stacking math in linear f32 (`florastack_core::convert`: exact round trips for every `u8` and `u16` value, sRGB through tables or a branch-free power function)

- [x] The synthetic generator's private exact blur and bicubic warp (`crates/core/src/testutil/ops.rs`) replaced in the generator by the public ops, which match them bit for bit, and kept as the tests' oracle

## Phase 2 — Alignment

### 2.1 Frame alignment *(plan first)*

- [x] Per-frame shift, scale, rotation and brightness gain relative to a master frame, each term switchable (`StackAligner`, which takes the frames one at a time and holds three prepared frames whatever the stack's length, and `align_frames`; `AlignSettings` with serde and a JSON schema; a term that is off stays exactly 0 or 1)
- [x] Coarse-to-fine ECC with a phase-correlation first guess, on band-passed luminance rather than gradient magnitudes: a linear band-pass keeps a symmetric defocus from shifting detail, and the gradient magnitude's apparent motion between frames focused apart was about twice as large. ECC gets a defocus term (the template's Laplacian, projected out) and Tukey weights; frames are chained to their neighbors, optionally refined against the master (which helps shallow stacks: 0.004 to 0.008 px instead of 0.008 to 0.019 without defocus, and hurts deep ones); frames above 2048 px are aligned at that size
- [x] Limits per term (shift in percent of the frame, scale change, rotation); alignments that hit a limit, score low or find no match are flagged and, by default, rejected, the chain going around them; repeating patterns are flagged from the peaks of the plain correlation, and the whitened one picks the period nearest the expected shift
- [x] Brightness gain from robust statistics of low-pass content: the median ratio to the master on a 64-pixel low-pass, within 0.04% of the truth without defocus
- [x] Synthetic-stack accuracy tests (`crates/align/tests/accuracy.rs`, 16 frames, three seeds, master first or in the middle), per stack: at most 0.3 px on noisy, dusty stacks (measured 0.18 to 0.21), as planned; at most 0.15 px on clean stacks (measured 0.09 to 0.12) instead of 0.1, because defocus alone moves the synthetic content by 0.06 to 0.12 px (the test's documentation explains why); and at most 0.05 px on stacks moved but not defocused (measured 0.008 to 0.019), which measures the aligner itself
- [x] Bench timing for a 100-frame 24 MP stack: `cargo xtask bench -p florastack-align`, `align/stack/100x6000x4000` 27.0 s on a 4-core VM (0.27 s per frame, 60% of it preparing the frame); `align/pair/*` shows shifts alone about 6% faster than every term
- [x] `align.run`: the engine command (a job) that reads and develops each frame, aligns the stack with `StackAligner` and stores each frame's `Alignment` and the `AlignmentRun` through a new `Edit` (`Edit::SetAlignment`), with the per-frame flags kept in the project (`Frame::report`, project format version 2); the apps get `AlignSettings` from the engine (`align.get`, `align.set`, `align.reset`). Built in 2.2, which needed it for sorting by scale and the out-of-focus flags
- [ ] Alignment checked on the real corpus stacks once they are in (an engine test: florastack-align can't read files)
- [ ] Reach 0.1 px on the clean synthetic set by modeling defocus that varies across the frame: one global Laplacian term leaves the uneven blur of receding surfaces and the depth edges
- [ ] The generator's defocus (`florastack_core::testutil`) blurs each pixel with its own depth's blur, by gathering: it doesn't conserve light near depth edges, and it moves depth edges with focus more than a lens does. Spreading each scene point with its own blur instead (scatter) would be closer to real optics; the stacking tests of Phase 3 will meet the same frames. In 3.1 they did: with no occlusion, the generator makes none of the halos and veils that a real subject's blurred edge spreads over its background, so its stacks always prefer the least smoothing, and the weighted average's defaults were chosen on the two local camera stacks instead. 3.2 added a generator of occluding layers (`generate_layered`, `scene::layered`), whose out-of-focus edges veil what lies behind them, and the stacking methods are scored on it; alignment's tests still use the gathering generator
- [ ] Prepare frame n + 1 while frame n is aligned (with 4.3's decoding of the next frame): preparing a 24 MP frame takes 0.16 s of its 0.27 s, mostly reading it and making the band-pass levels
- [ ] A frame rejected for a low score against its neighbor stays rejected even when refining against the master matches it well

### 2.2 Ordering, framing and edge handling

- [x] Narrow-end detection that sets the processing order, with automatic order and reverse order as overrides (`narrow_end`, `ProcessingOrder`, `AlignSettings::automatic_order` and `reverse_order`): the stack is aligned from its middle frame, then rebased onto the first frame that aligned at the narrow end (`StackAlignment::rebased`, `order_stack`), which becomes the master; `align.run` records the order, the narrow end and the narrow end's frame in the run's report
- [x] Framing: crop to the narrow end (default), keep the wide field of view with a coverage mask, or crop to what all frames cover (`Framing`, `Canvas`; the largest rectangle inside the intersection of the fields of view for `common`). The coverage mask is not stored: each frame's `Coverage` of the canvas follows from its transform, as a weight that ramps up over 2 pixels inside the frame's edge, and `warp_to_canvas` warps a frame onto a tile of the canvas with uncovered pixels 0 instead of smeared edge pixels; `align.info` gives the three canvases of the stored alignment, so changing the framing needs no new alignment
- [x] Sort by scale to repair out-of-order stacks, as a command (`align.sortByScale`, a job: a measuring pass in the present order, the frames sorted by scale in the direction of the present trend, aligned again in that order; the new order and the alignment are one undo step; `scale_order`, `align_by_scale`)
- [x] Out-of-focus frame detection (`Flag::OutOfFocus`, `FrameAlignment::sharp_fraction`): each frame's share of the stack's in-focus detail on which it is among the sharpest, from the gradient energy of its band-pass compared across the aligned frames; flagged below `min_sharp_fraction` (0.2%), never rejected, and shown in `project.frames` for the user to disable
- [x] Tests: a shuffled order is restored; keep-wide framing leaves no streaks (`crates/align/tests/ordering.rs`: coverage-weighted means of a wide canvas score 45.7 to 58.4 dB PSNR and 0.9986 to 0.9995 SSIM against the truth on the edge strips, where a clamped warp scores 25.7 to 32.3 dB and 0.83 to 0.91; `crates/engine/tests/align.rs` for the commands)
- [x] Checked on two local JPEG stacks (`corpus/local`, not committed; 15 and 23 frames): both narrow at their first, near end (20% and 22% of breathing over the stack); the farthest frame of one and four frames between the subject and the background of the other flagged out of focus (0 to 0.17% sharp content, against at least 1.8% for every other frame); sorting by scale moved one frame of the first stack, whose first frame has the scale of its third, after which every frame covers the whole narrow-end frame
- [x] Stacking (3.1) multiplies each frame's weights by its coverage, tile by tile (`warp_to_canvas`), and gives the pixels that no frame covers in wide framing (slivers along the edges that rotation leaves) an alpha of 0 and a color of 0 (`Stacked::alpha`; the output is stored with an alpha channel only when there are such pixels)
- [x] Slabs (3.4) carry the union of their frames' coverage: a slab's alpha is 1 where any of its frames covers the canvas, and the final stack of the slabs reads it as their coverage (tested in wide framing: the final stack leaves transparent exactly the pixels no frame covers). The union is all or nothing, as the stacker's alpha is: a slab's edge doesn't ramp as its frames' coverage does
- [ ] Narrow framing keeps the field of view of the frame at the narrow end, which on a stack shot out of order isn't the narrowest frame (in one of the local stacks, the second frame is narrower than the first): `align.run` should say so and suggest `align.sortByScale`
- [ ] A stale alignment: tell when the stored run no longer fits the project (develop or alignment settings changed since), before stacking uses it; a change of order settings alone could rebase the stored alignment instead of aligning again. Stacking (3.1) refuses frames added or enabled since the run (it asks for `align.run`), and leaves out frames disabled since and frames whose alignment was rejected
- [ ] Out-of-focus frames on the synthetic stacks: a frame focused 0.25 past the scene (2 pixels of blur on its farthest detail) keeps about 1% of sharp content, from cells where a near bristle crosses the far background, and isn't flagged; the generator's gathering blur (follow-up of 2.1) makes such depth edges unlike a lens's
- [ ] `align.sortByScale` reads and develops every frame twice; the measuring pass could keep each frame's smallest pyramid levels instead, at a few hundred kilobytes a frame
- [ ] Release-build timing of `align.run` on real frames: in the debug build, about 7 s per 20 MP camera JPEG, mostly decoding it

### 2.3 Dust, hot pixels and movement

- [x] Defect stage before warping (`DefectMask`, `DefectDetector`, `inpaint`): a user mask (an image of black spots on white, the frames' size) or one detected from the frames, inpainted in every frame by pull-push before it is aligned, and saved for reuse. Dust is darker than its surroundings on every side by the same factor in every frame: each pixel's ratio to the sixth darkest of 16 samples on a ring around it (12 px or 0.5% of the frame), whose mean and spread over the frames pick steady cores, grown by hysteresis, made symmetric about their center and grown by their radius; hot pixels are brighter than their neighbors two pixels away in 80% of the frames. At most 32 frames are read; the detector holds 10 bytes per pixel of the dust plane (half resolution above 4096 px) and 1 per frame pixel. In the engine: `defects.detect` (a job), `defects.import`, `defects.export` (an 8-bit PNG to edit), `defects.clear` and `defects.info`; the mask is the project's `defects` map, a lossless TIFF with its summary as cache key; `align.run` and `align.sortByScale` inpaint it, and detect one first when `detect_defects` is on (off by default) and the project has none, in the same undo step
- [x] Movement detection (`Motion`, `MotionMap`, `Flag::Moved`): each frame's residual motion against the neighbor it was aligned to, per cell (at least 8 px, at most 64 cells a side, on a level of at most 1024 px), by pyramidal Lucas–Kanade with the gain and defocus terms of the global alignment, on the band-pass pyramids alignment prepares anyway. A cell is measured when it has detail in two directions and correlates at least 0.8 once displaced; it moved from `min_motion` (2 px by default, and at least ¾ px of its level); moved cells count only where the frame is among the sharpest (its focus energy at least half the sharpest frame's), since elsewhere the stack takes that part from other frames; a region of two cells flags the frame, never rejects it. The project keeps each frame's score, largest displacement and regions (with their median displacement) in its report (format version 3), shown by `align.info`, `project.frames` (`motionScore`) and `filmstrip.list`
- [x] Filmstrip data API (`thumbnail`, `aligned_thumbnail`; `filmstrip.list`, `filmstrip.update`): every frame's preview as shot (developed and inpainted, at most 512 px, JPEG) and aligned onto the output's canvas of the framing set, keyed by what they were made from (the frame's contents, the develop settings and the defect mask; the preview, the transform and the canvas), so that the list tells current previews from stale ones. `align.run` writes them as it reads the frames, in its undo step; `filmstrip.update` (a superseding preview job, no undo step) makes the stale ones, the aligned ones from the previews without reading a frame. The project gets `Frame::aligned_preview`, `Edit::SetProjectMap`, `Edit::SetPreviews` and the `previews` aspect

## Phase 3 — Stacking engines

### 3.1 Streaming framework and weighted average

- [x] Streaming method trait with progress, tiled for large images and deterministic: `Stacker` (`begin`, `add_frame`, `finish`) over `FrameSource`s, aligned frames that hand out any rectangle of the canvas with its coverage (`Patch`), so that the engine warps each frame tile by tile as it is read and the warped frame is never whole. Methods are `TileKernel`s run by `Tiled`, which keeps their accumulators tile by tile (`TileGrid`, tiles at least four times the overlap, the overlap twice the kernel's reach) in a `TileCache` with a memory budget (`Tiling`: 512 px tiles and 1 GiB by default, spilled to temporary files beyond), on each tile's inner region (its core grown by half the overlap, all the stitch reads), folds each frame a batch of tiles at a time in parallel, and stitches the tiles back at the end. The same bit for bit on one thread or many, and on one tile or many for blurs of up to 4 px (box blurs beyond round their running sums differently, below 1e-5); `add_frame` and `finish` report tiles done to their `Monitor` and stop at the next batch when cancelled, which ends the stack (`FrameProgress` maps a frame's tiles onto the whole stack's progress). `Method` makes stackers from settings, and `stack` drives one over frames in memory
- [x] Weighted-average method (`AverageSettings`, `WeightedAverage`): per pixel and frame, the Laplacian's energy of the frame's luminance (Nayar and Nakagawa's focus measure, the energy-of-Laplacian form of Pertuz et al.'s comparison) averaged over `radius`, scaled, floored and raised to `power`, blurred over `smoothing` and multiplied by the frame's coverage (its Laplacian left out where it would read an uncovered pixel), normalized and blended (contrast weights raised to an exponent, as Mertens et al.'s exposure fusion weighs its images, but blended directly). Defaults radius 3, smoothing 8, power 3, chosen on the two local camera stacks: a radius of 1 left a veil over the cricket's compound eye, and a smoothing of 3 dark dots and ghost outlines in the smooth background around the wasp
- [x] Quarter-scale preview mode for every method (`Preview`, `preview`, `Method::reduced`: frames and their coverage reduced by Lanczos3, lengths in the settings divided by 4)
- [x] Tests (`crates/stack/tests/average.rs`, `memory.rs`): beats the best single frame on SSIM, at a camera's scale (the scene enlarged three times, 720x540) with the defaults, 0.923 to 0.924 against 0.897 to 0.909 clean and 0.877 to 0.878 against 0.848 to 0.861 noisy, and at the generator's own (320x240) with settings for it, 0.961 to 0.962 against 0.894 to 0.895 and 0.878 to 0.879 against 0.869 to 0.870; frames warped onto the scene with their coverage, 0.951 to 0.956 against 0.887 to 0.890; uncovered pixels never smear (a frame's streaked edge beyond its coverage leaves no trace) and get alpha 0; the preview beats the best reduced frame (0.896 against 0.870) and is 38 dB from the full stack reduced; peak heap flat from 20 to 200 frames: 21.76 MiB for both (1520 bytes apart, a one-time allocation of the test harness), counted by dhat's allocator in a test binary of its own, with a 640x480 frame of 3.5 MiB in flight; plus tiles against one tile, threads, spilling to disk, cancellation, progress, hostile samples and settings

### 3.2 Pyramid method *(plan first)*

- [x] Laplacian pyramid and complex wavelets evaluated on the metrics and one chosen: the Laplacian pyramid (P. J. Burt and E. H. Adelson, 1983, with the 5-tap binomial kernel). In a throwaway prototype outside the repository, on layered synthetic stacks (below; 12 frames, three seeds), it scored SSIM 0.967 and halo 0.080 at a camera's scale on clean stacks, 0.956 and 0.080 on noisy ones, against 0.901 and 0.134 (clean) for complex wavelets after B. Forster et al. (2004) as focus-stack implements them (MIT, commit `0cd289e`), 0.956 and 0.082 (clean) and 0.922 and 0.083 (noisy) for the best wavelet variant (given the pyramid's window, blend and range limit), and 0.921 and 0.132, 0.894 and 0.133 for the weighted average; the wavelets were 2.5 to 4 times slower, need the image padded to a multiple of 128 pixels, and ring in flat dark areas. Of focus-stack only the range limit's idea is taken (ATTRIBUTION.md, NOTICE)
- [x] Streaming selection or soft blend of the highest-energy coefficients per level (`PyramidSettings`, `Pyramid`, `crates/stack/src/pyramid/`): at every level and position, the coefficients of the frame with the most local energy (the 5x5 binomial energy of the band's luminance, P. J. Burt and R. J. Kolczynski's salience), kept with that energy as a running best-so-far pyramid; the residual averaged over the frames, weighted by coverage; frames that cover part of the canvas reduced by normalized convolution (Knutsson and Westin), their choices weighed by the fourth power of their pooled coverage. Levels: `levels`, or by default `log2(short side) - 4` (5 at 720x540, 7 at 20 MP; the prototype scored 5 to 8 alike). The finest 3 levels are computed in tiles (a context of 48 pixels, reads aligned to 8 pixels so that every tile's levels lie on the canvas's lattice), the coarser ones and the residual on the whole canvas at 1/8 scale from each tile's share: `TileKernel` gained the hooks for it (`read_region`, `start`, `absorb`, `prepare`, state planes of `f32` or `u16`), and the weighted average runs on them unchanged. The same bit for bit on one tile or many, one thread or many, spilled or not (tested). State: 33 bytes per pixel for RGB (the weighted average's 16), plus 1/64 of the canvas per plane for the coarse part
- [x] Grit suppression: the finest level is a soft blend, each frame's coefficients weighted by its energy raised to `1 / grit` (`grit` 0.5 by default; 0 picks, as the other levels do; 0.25 to 1 blend). On the layered noisy stacks the blend has 0.0040 of flat-region noise against 0.0082 for picking and 0.0047 for the weighted average, for 0.0003 of SSIM on clean stacks. The prompt's consistency check of the fine level's choices against the coarser level's choice map was measured in the prototype (selecting where the two agree, blending elsewhere: noise 0.0047 against the blend's 0.0040 on noisy stacks, for 20 more bytes of state per pixel) and, with the user's approval, not built
- [x] Unclipped float result with a final local tone compression, and a "retain extended range" output: the stack's result is unclipped; `compress_range` (`Tone`: `compress`, the default, a shoulder whose knee follows the local maximum over 32-pixel blocks, so that only pixels near values above 1 change, and negatives to 0; `clip`; `keep`) brings it into range. `stack.pyramid` keeps the result from before tone compression as the output's `extended` map when that changes any pixel, and `output.save` writes it for `extended_range` at 32-bit float (TIFF, OpenEXR); codecs wrote float files with extended range since 1.2
- [x] Decisions on luminance (`Decisions::Luminance`, the default: one choice per position for all channels) or on all channels (`Decisions::Channels`). On the synthetic stacks, which have no chromatic aberration and colored texture at constant luminance, channel decisions scored higher (SSIM 0.971 against 0.960, mean deltaE 1.79 against 2.04 on the noisy native-scale stack); on the wasp they put blue-violet fringes along the wing and the yellow markings, so luminance stays the default
- [x] Limits on inversion halos: the range limit (`range_limit`, 1 by default), each channel of each pixel held within the range of the frames that cover it (after focus-stack's gray range limiting, per channel, its bounds kept as bfloat16 rounded outward): no pixel comes out darker or brighter than every frame. On the wasp, a luminance-only limit left 1.6% of the pixels out of range and a blue cast on the black thorax; per channel, 0.02% and neutral blacks (prototype, quarter scale). The prompt's limit on how far coarse choices may stray from the depth-consistent frame is built as `coarse_limit` (frames; at levels 3 and up; the depth-consistent frame is the one whose finest-level energy, pooled to the level, is largest there) and, with the user's approval, off by default: every form of it raised the halo metric on the layered stacks (0.082 against 0.080; with the true depth at levels 3 and up 0.099 against 0.077, at every level, an ideal depth map, 0.184), since at an occluding edge the frame in focus on the background is the one its out-of-focus foreground veils, and it showed no gain on the local stacks

### 3.3 Depth-map method

- [x] Focus measure (modified Laplacian or Tenengrad) with a per-pixel best frame and a confidence (`DepthSettings`, `Estimator`, `FocusKernel`, `crates/stack/src/depth/`): pass 1 folds each frame's focus measure, the energy of the modified Laplacian (S. K. Nayar and Y. Nakagawa, 1994) of the luminance blurred over `radius` and averaged over `radius`, into a running record per pixel kept tile by tile (32 bytes a pixel): the sharpest frame, its focus and its neighbors' (the peak refined between frames by Nayar and Nakagawa's Gaussian interpolation), the runner-up (the most focus at least two frames from the sharpest, kept exactly as the sharpest moves), the floor (the least focus) and the sharpest frame's luminance. Confidence: the sharpest frame's local contrast less the runner-up's, `sqrt(peak) - sqrt(runner)`, and 0 where the focus curve never falls to a quarter of its peak (noise, a smooth surface). Measured in a throwaway sweep on synthetic stacks: without the blur before the Laplacian, the noise of camera-scale frames (twelve times the fine texture's energy) picked the sharpest frame at random (depth correlation 0.08 to 0.13 on noisy stacks), with it 0.56 to 0.69; a radius of 2 at camera scale was best of 1.5 to 4.5 (default 2); the mean of the other frames as the runner-up scored the same as the prompt's runner-up, kept
- [x] Edge-aware smoothing, with low-contrast regions filled from confident neighbors (`FocusStats::resolve`, `resolve.rs`, `filter.rs`): the confident pixels (at or above the threshold, not masked, and with a confident majority of their neighborhood) smoothed by the domain transform's recursive filter (E. S. L. Gastal and M. M. Oliveira, 2011) as a normalized convolution, guided by the cube root of the sharpest frames' luminance (`EDGE` 0.05: at 0.1 a smoothing of 16 px raised the halo metric from 0.09 to 0.12), and the rest filled by push-pull (S. J. Gortler et al., 1996) where fewer than half of a pixel's neighborhood is confident. All-or-nothing weights beat confidence-weighted ones in every synthetic case (layered clean SSIM 0.961 against 0.953)
- [x] Compositing from interpolated sub-frame depth that never alters color or contrast (`DepthMap::compositor`, `CompositeKernel`, `composite.rs`): pass 2 reads every frame again and takes each pixel from the two frames on either side of its depth, in proportion (weights `max(0, 1 - |i - depth|)` times coverage); where neither covers it (wide framing), the nearest covering frame's pixel. Every sample lies within the frames' range at its pixel (tested to 1e-6), and frames alike stack to themselves
- [x] Two-pass API: cached focus statistics, so a new threshold re-composites in under a second: `Estimator` (begin, add_frame, finish) gives `FocusStats` (depth, confidence, guide: 12 bytes a pixel); `FocusStats::resolve(threshold, smoothing, mask)` gives a `DepthMap` without reading a frame; its `compositor()` is a `Stacker` for pass 2; `depth_stack` runs all three on frames in memory. On 12 frames of 720x540 in memory, a new threshold takes 28 to 33 ms and its composite with it 60 to 68 ms (the fastest of three; tested under a second), and give the same depth map and composite as a whole new stack, bit for bit. At 24 MP the depth map alone takes 1.85 s (bench below), over the second: the interactive threshold belongs on the quarter-scale preview, which a sixteenth of the pixels make a fraction of a second
- [x] Preset thresholds (absolute or percentile) for batch runs, and an optional low-confidence mask: `threshold` = `auto` (the default), `level` (the absolute `level`, 0.001 by default) or `percentile` (`percentile` percent of the pixels, 30 by default), stored as three keys so that presets merge key by key (a threshold stored as `{ level = ... }` or `{ percentile = ... }`, as first written, merged into an object with both and failed to read back: regression-tested). `auto`, added for the real stacks: Otsu's level on the logarithm of the confidence (N. Otsu, 1979), taken when the pixels below it have depth that follows noise (median distance to the nearest-agreeing of four neighbors a few windows away at least 0.5 frames and four times the pixels above's), the 10th percentile otherwise. A fixed percentile of 30 filled a third of the wasp's canvas (below), whose smooth background is three quarters of it, and left seams and dark specks around its legs; Otsu's level alone filled 79% of a synthetic stack with texture everywhere and cost it 0.07 of SSIM. The mask (one channel, at least 0.5 to mark) forces its pixels low-confidence (tested: a masked square's depth is filled from around, 0.10 frames from the truth)
- [x] Outputs: the composite, a 16-bit depth map and the confidence map: `DepthMap::depth_u16` (the frame index scaled, 0 for the first frame, 65535 for the last) and `confidence_u16` (`c / (c + level)`, 0.5 at the threshold); the engine keeps them as the output's `depth` and `confidence` maps (16-bit TIFF in the cache), and `output.save` writes any map with `map` (profile `untagged` required: maps are data)
- [x] Tests: depth correlates with ground truth, deltaE near zero, fast threshold changes (`crates/stack/tests/depth.rs`, `memory.rs`): on a textured backdrop on a sloped, undulating depth at camera scale, the depth map's correlation with the truth is 1.000 clean and 0.999 noisy (mean error 0.011 and 0.068 frames; pass 1 alone 0.996 and 0.984); on `scene::textured` (flat discs, thin bristles) 0.63 and 0.63; mean CIEDE2000 against the truth 0.19 on the sloped stack, and below the pyramid's and the weighted average's on clean layered stacks (1.67 against 1.98 and 3.08 textured, 1.02 against 1.11 and 1.62 smooth); against the weighted average on layered stacks, higher SSIM on the textured backdrop (0.929 to 0.961 against 0.896 to 0.922) and less halo everywhere (0.074 to 0.093 against 0.119 to 0.129); the pyramid stays sharper (0.955 to 0.981) where near and far detail overlap, and the depth map's flat-region noise is one or two frames' (0.0068 against the pyramid's 0.0039 on noisy stacks). ### 3.4 Slabbing and very deep stacks

- [x] Slabbing that wraps any pair of methods, with slab size and overlap defaulting from the frame count, and slab outputs kept as project outputs (`stack.slabbed`, `crates/engine/src/commands/slab.rs`): the frames, in the order they are stacked, split into slabs of `slabSize` consecutive frames, neighbors sharing at least `overlap`, spaced evenly from the first frame to the last (`layout`); by default a slab holds the square root of the number of frames, rounded up (18 for 300 frames: 22 slabs), which balances the two stages, and neighbors share a fifth of a slab, at least one frame. The slabs' method (`slabs`) and the final method (`final`), the pyramid for both by default, take `stack.preview`'s method parameters on top of the project's settings. Slabs are stacked one at a time, in the memory of one stack, and stored as they finish: each an output of its own (`"slab": {"index", "of"}` in its parameters, its frames as its inputs), keyed by everything it is made from (the method and its settings, each frame's file, development, defect mask, transform and gain, the canvas, FloraStack's version), so that with `reuse` (the default) a slab that an output already has is taken from it and not stacked again: a new setting of the final stack reads only the slabs' outputs. The final stage reads them back with their alpha as coverage and a pyramid slab's result before tone compression, compressing once at the end with the pyramid's `tone`; the final output's inputs are the slabs' outputs, all in one undo step, and a reused output removed meanwhile is added again. The first version kept two slabs open to read the frames they share once, each with half of the 1 GiB tile budget: at 20 MP the pyramid's 860 MB of accumulators spilled to disk at every frame, and the wasp's slabs took 476 s
- [x] Automatic splitting of a folder of several stacks by jumps in framing or scale, or gaps in capture time (`project.findStacks`, `crates/engine/src/commands/split.rs`; `link`, `boundaries`, `SplitSettings`, `crates/align/src/split.rs`): each file read once, in order, reduced to a 512-pixel thumbnail and compared with the one before; a new stack starts where capture times are further apart than `gap` (by default 30 s, or 8 times the batch's median gap when that is longer), where the next frame shows another scene (alignment's score below 0.3 or no match, and the correlation of the frames' low-passed luminance below 0.9: defocus keeps local averages, so blurred neighbors of one stack still look alike), where the scale jumps by more than 5% or the view by more than 10% of the diagonal between frames that match, or where the frame size changes. It needs no project, lists each stack's files and every boundary with its reasons, and skips files it can't read without cutting their stack
- [x] Tests: slabbed pyramid then depth map on 300 frames matches unslabbed quality and runs faster; three concatenated stacks are split apart (`crates/engine/tests/slab.rs`, `split.rs`; `crates/align/tests/split.rs`). On 300 frames of a layered scene (200x150, each frame sharp over a few frames, in register), with the depth map's settings at the scene's scale for every depth map: pyramid slabs then a depth map score SSIM 0.8679, against the unslabbed pyramid's 0.8857 (tested within 0.03) and the unslabbed depth map's 0.7333 (tested at least 0.08 above); the default, a pyramid of the pyramid slabs, 0.8961. A new depth-map setting reprocesses the 22 slabs in 0.94 s, against 9.32 s for an unslabbed depth map and 5.24 s for a pyramid of the frames (tested four and three times faster). The first run is not faster: 9.49 s, 1.81 times the pyramid (tested under three times), since it reads and stacks the frames neighbors share for each slab (396 frames) and finishes and stores 22 slabs. Three concatenated synthetic stacks (a scene, the same scene framed 25% larger, another scene) are cut at frames 8 and 16, both as other scenes (the reframed stack matches at 0.17, and its low-passed luminance correlates 0.84, against at least 0.97 within a stack); a 12% reframing is cut as a scale jump, a move of a fifth of the frame as a shift, and neighbors of clean and noisy stacks stay together. Plus the layout and its defaults, reuse (all, none, a removed slab, the slabs around a disabled frame), one undo step, coverage through depth-map slabs in wide framing (each slab transparent where its frames are, the final stack only where no frame is), the color space kept, refusals, cancellation, capture times with and without an offset, and the gap rules
- [x] Checked on the local JPEG stacks (`corpus/local`, not committed; release build, 20 MP, 16 threads): `project.findStacks` over the five folders found their five stacks, [15, 23, 8, 16, 8] frames, in 20.0 s for 70 frames, each boundary for two reasons (a gap of days in capture time, and another scene or another frame size); within the stacks every neighbor matched with a score of at least 0.96 and a low-passed correlation of at least 0.938 (the wasp, where it moved), with scale steps up to 2.3% and shifts up to 3.0%. Slabbing the wasp (23 frames: 6 slabs of 5, sharing 1): the first run took 137.5 s, against 52.2 s for the pyramid and 33.0 s for the depth map, and a new final stack 21.6 s (a depth map) or 18.9 s (a pyramid). A pyramid of the slabs is as clean as the pyramid of the frames at 100%; a depth map of them filled patches of a leg's smooth inside from the blurred slabs behind it and left dark specks along the abdomen's edge, which made the pyramid the default final method (follow-up below)
- [ ] A depth map of few slabs: on the wasp's six slabs, at the depth map's defaults, the automatic threshold came out at 0 (the 10th percentile: 64% of the pixels have no confidence, their focus never falling to a quarter of its peak over six slabs), and the fill took depth from the blurred slabs into a leg; the depth-map method's assumptions (a runner-up two frames away, a focus curve over many frames) fit slabs only when there are many. Score it on real slabs before making it a default again
- [ ] The first run's cost: on shallow stacks the slabs' own work outweighs the frames' (the wasp's first run 2.6 times the pyramid's): each slab is finished (the pyramid's collapse), stored as a deflated 32-bit float TIFF of the whole canvas (240 MB at 20 MP, with its `extended` map when out of range) and read back by the final stage. A faster lossless format for slabs, or finishing a slab while the next one starts, would cut it; so would keeping the frames neighbors share in memory instead of reading them again (each a whole frame)
- [ ] Disk: every slab is an output of the whole canvas, so slabs take the frames' space again (the wasp's project grew to 2.5 GB, a 300-frame 24 MP stack's 22 slabs about 6 GB). Store slabs at 16 bits or half floats, or offer to drop them once the final output is kept
- [ ] Alignment of very deep stacks: on the 300-frame synthetic stack, chained over many frames with next to no detail in common, alignment with every term drifted to a scale of 0.90 and a rotation of -1.8 degrees and rejected 50 frames at the scale limit (the slabbing test aligns with the terms off, its frames being in register). Aligning each frame against a keyframe with enough detail, or limiting the drift per frame, would hold it
- [ ] Slab coverage is all or nothing (the stacker's alpha): a slab's edge in wide framing doesn't ramp as its frames' coverage does, so the final stack can show a slab's edge where the others don't cover
- [ ] Splitting: a new framing of the same subject by 25% is reported as another scene, since alignment from a scale of 1 doesn't reach it (a 12% one is measured as a scale jump); a log-polar first guess would measure the scale. Retakes of the same subject and framing with no capture times aren't separated; the focus jumping back from the far end to the near end would tell them
- [ ] Splitting decodes every RAW file in full (with the fast demosaic); its embedded preview would do for comparing neighbors
- [ ] The CLI's `batch` and auto-split (4.1, 4.2) and the app's import (5.1) on `project.findStacks`; retouching from slabs (5.3)

### 3.5 Quality report and regression gate

- [x] `cargo xtask quality`: every method and slab combination on every corpus stack, with metrics, runtime and peak memory, in an HTML report with 100% crops (`xtask/src/quality/`; the runner, `crates/engine/examples/quality/`). xtask, which depends on no FloraStack crate, builds florastack-engine's `quality` example and runs it once per stack, each in a process of its own; the runner drives the engine's commands as the apps do: alignment, the weighted average, the pyramid and the depth map, then slabs by each method stacked by each method (`stack.slabbed`; one slab method's three final stacks in a row, the first stacking the slabs and the other two reusing them, as exploring does), at the defaults or at `--settings` (`stack.set`'s parameters). Five synthetic stacks at a camera's scale (`synthetic.rs`: a layered scene clean, noisy, and noisy over a smooth backdrop, 480x360 and 12 frames in register; a textured scene whose 12 frames breathe, shift, rotate and change in brightness, with noise and dust; a layered scene of 48 frames, each sharp over a few), scored against their truth brought onto the output's canvas (SSIM, PSNR, halo, noise, color shift, the share of pixels out of range) and their alignment against their transforms (mean transform error, rejected frames); the manifest's downloaded stacks and the local ones, scored against their `reference` when they have one. Runtime is the command's wall time; peak memory is the most heap the command held beyond what was allocated when it started, counted by `peak_alloc`'s global allocator (two atomics: dhat's lock and backtrace on every allocation would slow what is timed), on one thread for the synthetic stacks so that it is the same everywhere. `target/quality/index.html`: the verdict, every run's measures with their change from the baseline, and three regions of every output side by side at 100% of the screen's pixels (or 200% and 400%), the truth first; the regions are chosen on the three methods' outputs (where their detail differs most, where the worst is furthest from the truth or, without one, where their tones differ most, and where the truth or the results have the most detail), and each output is deleted once cut, so that a 20 MP stack's 30 outputs (7 GB) never fill the disk. Measured: the synthetic stacks in 1.5 min (dev profile, one thread); the five local JPEG stacks (`corpus/local`, not committed; 20 MP, 8 to 23 frames; the report kept in the scratchpad) in 46 min (dev profile, 8 threads, 6.6 to 12.7 min a stack; the stacking commands' peak heap 1.1 to 1.4 GiB, the 1 GiB tile budget with a frame and scratch, alignment's 0.46 GiB), and the two compressed-ORF stacks stop at alignment with the frame florastack-raw can't decode, which the report shows
- [x] A baseline JSON in the repository; CI fails on regressions beyond tolerance unless the same commit updates the baseline (`quality/baseline.json`, `xtask/src/quality/gate.rs`; CI's Quality gate step, and `quality` in `cargo xtask ci`): every run of the synthetic stacks and of the manifest's downloaded ones, never a local one, 65 runs, with each measure's tolerance, the larger of an absolute amount and a share of the baseline's value (SSIM 0.002, PSNR 0.1 dB, halo 0.003, noise 5%, color shift 0.05, out of range 0.001, alignment 0.02 px, rejected frames 0, peak heap 10% or 0.5 MiB, runtime twice the baseline and 0.5 s). `cargo xtask quality --gate` runs the baseline's stacks in its build profile (the dev profile, which `cargo test` has built) and fails on a measure worse than the baseline beyond its tolerance, a run that fails or no longer runs, or a measure no longer measured; improvements and new runs are listed. Runtimes count only against a baseline made on the same machine with the same build profile, so CI gates every measure but time. `cargo xtask quality --update-baseline` rewrites it, keeping the tolerances, in the commit that moves what it measures. Two runs on one machine give the same values to the baseline's six significant digits, peak memory included, and runtimes within 54% (one second on one thread: the reason a run only fails at twice its time). A degraded setting fails the gate: `--settings '{"pyramid": {"grit": 0}}'` on 62 measures of 29 of the 30 runs with a pyramid in them (noise up to twice the baseline's, SSIM down by up to 0.033); and lowering the pyramid's default `grit` to 0.25 in the code fails it on 46 measures of 24 runs (noise up 31% and 38% on the noisy stacks, SSIM down 0.007 and 0.009). To keep the gate quick, the dev profile now optimizes the crates that read, write and hash image files as well (florastack-codecs and -project, LightCraft's tiff, codecs and color, and the Deflate, PNG, TIFF, JPEG, color and SHA-256 crates): one synthetic stack's 13 runs took 49 s without them and 16 s with, with the same results
- [ ] Runtime in CI: the baseline's runtimes are one machine's, so CI gates quality and memory only. Runtimes divided by a calibration workload timed in the same run, or a baseline per CI runner, would let CI gate time too
- [ ] Disk traffic isn't measured: the heap leaves out the tile cache's spill files, which 3.4's first slabbing (476 s against 52) would have shown; counting the bytes spilled and read back would let the gate catch it on any machine
- [ ] Alignment drifts on stacks whose frames don't move: a mean transform error of 0.52 px on the clean layered stack, 1.25 px with noise and 2.55 px over 48 frames (`align_error` in the baseline), likely defocus moving the content (0.06 to 0.12 px a frame, AGENTS.md) chained frame to frame; it costs the stacks some of their score.
